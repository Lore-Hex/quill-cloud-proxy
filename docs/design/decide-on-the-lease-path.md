## Baseline and latency budget

Supplied measurement, 2026-09-24, keep-alive from us-central1: hosted `typesafe-ai/jev` takes 553 ms end to end through the us-central1 enclave, with approximately 100 ms in the vendor and approximately 453 ms attributed to the control plane. A1 separately targets a 200–300 ms enclave/control-plane round trip from each region.

Today hosted decide calls `AuthorizeEmbeddingsWithRoute`, invokes the vendor, verifies, then calls `Settle` before writing JSON. Native decide constructs a chat request, enters `runFusionCallValidated`, authorizes through `authorizeFusionCall`, collects and validates the generation, then waits for `settleAndBroadcast`. (`E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:390`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:488`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:529`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:599`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/fusion.go:1973`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/fusion.go:2084`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/fusion.go:2123`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/fusion.go:2722`.)

Let R be a successful reserve latency, M the entire provider attempt including any fallback, S the settlement wait, and L local validation, buffering, encoding and scheduling. Exclude client/enclave transit from these equations. For a warm eligible attempt:

| Mode | Time until answer can be written | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Today | R + M + S + L | Two serial control-plane waits |
| Step 1a only | max(R, M) + S + L | Speculation overlaps reserve, settlement still blocks |
| Step 1a + 1b | max(R, M) + L | Reserve still gates the first byte |
| Step 2, optional | M + L | No per-request router acknowledgement before reply |

For M = 100 ms and R = 200–300 ms after A1, Step 1a + 1b is approximately 200–300 ms plus L and client transit, **not 101 ms**. Its added critical-path latency over the model is approximately 100–200 ms plus L. If M exceeds R, Step 1 can hide R, but that is workload dependent. If each of today's two waits were 200–300 ms, the corresponding serial budget would be 500–700 ms; that is an illustration, not a forecast replacing the 553 ms observation. Two native attempts have two admission/provider timelines and two billing records; the single-attempt formula is not a retry-path SLO.

Define the desired ~1 ms as *warm-path TrustedRouter processing overhead relative to identical provider work*, including local admission and verification, not total decide time or internet transit. Measure M, R, their overlap, post-model gate wait, settlement start/durability/finalization, local CPU and response-write time independently at p50/p95/p99. Report warm lease hits separately from cold start, rotation, policy miss and ordinary authorization. Serialization of a large state and cryptographic signing might themselves exceed 1 ms; benchmark before promising it.

## What the existing lease protocol actually guarantees

Stage C is not offline billing authority. `PrepareSpendLeaseAdmission` performs local eligibility/credential checks, uses the signed catalog and conservatively decrements local remaining capacity; nil means ordinary synchronous authorization **before** provider work. `ReserveSpendLeaseAdmission` submits the signed receipt, binds an authorization at the router, and checks the acceptance marker, receipt hash, remaining amount, identity and ordered dispatch candidates. Only that marked response opens the gate. The chat implementation cancels speculation on errors and on unmarked responses. (`E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/stage_c.go:34`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/stage_c.go:151`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/stage_c.go:196`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/stage_c.go:223`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/main.go:1230`.)

Local admission requires authoritative/local-admission claims, the registered boot key, matching policy hash, an active grant and an unexpired admission window. CAS decrements are never restored, even on cancellation/refusal. `Release` releases only the in-flight idempotency scope. Reserve observations only reduce remaining capacity. A signed token alone, or a `spend_lease_echo`, is not permission to return an answer. (`E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:149`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:173`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:205`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:253`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/types.go:136`.)

Current tokens have a maximum 60-second TTL, a 28-second admission margin/reserve budget and 10-second skew; verification constructs monotonic deadlines and binds identity. The router verifies the boot, receipt, lease generation, policy, catalog estimate and acceptance flag, then reserves through the typed binding machinery. Preserve byte-stable retries and the original billing authority, but fix the shared receipt-aware replay defect described below before relying on lost-ack recovery. (`E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/types.go:9`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/verifier.go:95`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/verifier.go:112`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/verifier.go:149`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/spend_lease.go:183`, `R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:2741`, `R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:1586`.)

Stage D is narrower still. Router eligibility requires streaming Chat/Responses, Credits, standard pricing, accepted boot and supported settlement backend; priority/auto are excluded. Enclave eligibility repeats the public stream/route gate. It sends a pre-header heartbeat, meters slices, terminates at cap or heartbeat loss, and makes a bounded settle attempt before terminal output. Heartbeats atomically validate endpoint, monotonic usage/sequence and cap, store delivered usage and renew the hold. Decide cannot obtain these guarantees by passing through a chat helper. (`R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:3103`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_stage_d.py:47`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_stage_d.py:91`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_stage_d.py:121`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/stage_d.go:50`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/stage_d.go:157`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/stage_d.go:198`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/main.go:1890`.)

### Native attempt lifecycle

Build and validate `decide.NativeChatRequest` first, including prompt/schema, reasoning options, provider constraints and explicit token budget; normalize the same reasoning cap as `authorizeFusionCall` before preparing admission. The builder caps output at 32,768 tokens, but its prompt estimate is not a hard billing upper bound (`E:enclave-go/internal/decide/chat.go:269`, `E:enclave-go/internal/decide/chat.go:274`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/fusion.go:2714`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/client.go:1451`).

For **each** of at most two attempts:

1. Assign its idempotency key before preparation. Keep attempt 1's key and attempt 2's `:decide-retry-2` suffix; generate a nonempty key when absent. Freeze it through reserve transport retries, settlement and repair. Do not use attempt 1's allocation for attempt 2. Current retry/billing semantics are explicit at `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:590`.
2. Prepare using route `decide`. On a miss, use today's synchronous `authorizeFusionCall`. On a plan, start the ordinary provider fallback machinery with `plan.Local` concurrently with reserve. Refactor the fusion helper into authorize / invoke-and-collect / prepare-finalization / finalize seams; select this mode only for decide, not all fusion callers.
3. Collect into a bounded, private buffer while reserve runs. Do not merely leave the existing `io.Pipe` unread: it can block the first provider write and prevent whole-model overlap. Allocate a fresh **execution-specific** generation recorder, collector/buffer, selected-route tracker, upstream usage/counts, cancellation and finalization owner. An attempt may contain two executions: speculation, then an unmarked-response redispatch. Each execution respects the same overall attempt deadline and limits; redispatch does not reset the request budget. Never attach the speculative writer to the client socket. Match the selected endpoint against the accepted frozen candidates before using the result.
4. After accepted reserve (or ordinary authorization for a synchronous execution), apply that execution's `recorder.complete` to its own collector result **before deciding settle versus refund**. Incomplete/error/empty generations are refunded and may consume the remaining attempt. A complete generation is billed even when `ExtractNative`/`Verify` rejects its answer; those checks happen before any public answer but do not turn native malformed answers into free calls. Accumulate customer usage only from billable executions of completed attempts and retain each execution's immutable finalization job. Two invalid completed attempts yield a spent error. Control-plane verdicts do not consume the second attempt as a workaround. (`E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:124`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:604`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:632`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/fusion.go:2072`.)

Keep native provider fallback before its first provider byte; after a stream has committed, a broken generation follows the existing refund/new-attempt rules. An ordered candidate change inside an attempt uses the same authorization, not a new lease allocation. The outer validation retry is a new billed attempt. These are different counters and must stay different (`E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:74`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:600`).

### Execution isolation and finalization ownership

An attempt owns its idempotency key, reserve operation and overall budget; an execution owns provider state and its eventual settle/refund decision. Pre-first-byte candidate fallback remains within one execution. A marked reserve attaches the returned authorization to the speculative execution exactly once. An unmarked authorization does **not** attach to that execution: cancel it, close/drain its pipe, retire its recorder, collector, route tracker and finalization capability, and join its writers or fence every callback by execution ID before replacement. Late callbacks must never write into replacement state or enqueue settlement/refund for the returned authorization. Do not reset and reuse a recorder while old writers may still hold it.

After validating the unmarked ordinary authorization, create a new execution with a new recorder (`sawStop=false`), empty collector/result slot, fresh selected-route and usage state, and a new finalization owner **under that returned authorization and its pinned control-plane endpoint**. Dispatch only its candidates; do not reauthorize, allocate another lease, or change the attempt key. Only this replacement execution can settle/refund that authorization. A cancellation before replacement starts still needs one refund owner for the unused returned authorization; coordinate this with dispatch so there cannot be competing owners. The speculative execution has no customer billing authority and cannot settle or refund the replacement's hold.

Retain discarded-execution facts separately, keyed by attempt and execution: completion/spent status, observed usage, attempted routes and estimated vendor expense, including late completion evidence. They feed operator expense accounting and the final `x-should-retry:false` decision, never replacement completeness, customer usage, selected response metadata or settlement payloads. Hosted executions use the same ownership rule with fresh bounded result/validation state. Current recorder writes become no-ops after `sawStop`, and the collector can return partial text at clean EOF; these facts make fresh state mandatory (`E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:94`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:135`, `E:enclave-go/internal/adapter/responses.go:722`).

Required combined regression: **`TestDecideUnmarkedRedispatchAfterSpeculativeStopPartialCleanEOFRefunds`**. Speculation completes with text and sets `sawStop`; reserve returns a valid unmarked ordinary authorization; replacement emits nonempty partial text then clean EOF without a terminal event. Its fresh recorder must reject completion and **refund the returned authorization exactly once, with no settlement for either execution**. Any subsequent native attempt has a new key/authorization and is accounted independently. If the request ends in error, the completed discarded execution requires `x-should-retry:false`; retain its expense facts even though the customer hold was refunded. Also exercise late speculative writes/callbacks after retirement and cancellation at the ownership transfer.

### Reserve gate outcomes, including races

| Outcome | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Local miss/signing failure/expired or exhausted grant | No speculation; ordinary authorization first. Local decrement, if already made, remains spent. |
| Marked, matching accepted reserve | Adopt the returned authorization ID and pinned control-plane endpoint. Reuse buffered result/provider stream; never dispatch it a second time. |
| Explicit refusal, admission/replay 409, malformed marker or timeout/unreachable router | Cancel/retire provider execution, close/drain buffers and discard answer. Preserve the classified control-plane failure; no fallback to an unreserved public success. Only bounded transport failures retry the exact receipt/body/proof under the same invocation, using receipt-aware replay validation below; never settle a local authorization with an empty ID. |
| Valid but unmarked authorization from older router | Retire speculation and its finalization capability; validate/adopt the ordinary returned authorization and start a fresh execution from its candidates as above. A replay without receipt proof must still pass ordinary nonce/claim validation; an unmarked replay with absent/mismatched nonce is a 409, not permission to redispatch. |
| Provider fails/completes before reserve | Store result/error until reserve classification. If an authorization is obtained, assign it only to the eligible execution and finalize it exactly once according to the above rules; if none is obtained, account speculative vendor loss operationally. |
| Client disconnects during either operation | Cancel unnecessary provider work; reconcile any returned authorization with detached finalization. A completed accepted generation remains billable under today's native/hosted rules. |

These follow the chat precedent at `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/main.go:1234` and marker checks at `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/stage_c.go:174`. Cancellation is not proof the vendor stopped or waived charges. Keep explicit 401/402/429 and Retry-After semantics, and expose the admission/replay 409 contract below; if a complete discarded generation makes a final error unsafe to regenerate, attach `x-should-retry:false` without changing its status/source. That header means provider work was spent, **not** that customer settlement committed (`E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:188`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/http_io.go:696`). Do not turn a reserve refusal into a second native attempt. An unresolved accepted hold may still reap unbilled after retry exhaustion, expiry or loss of invocation state; the deterministic lost-ack nonce mismatch below is a defect to fix, not an accepted ambiguity.

### Shared Stage C prerequisite: receipt-aware lost-ack replay

**Existing chat finding; fix first, independently of decide.** The shared decoder generates a nonempty invocation nonce and rejects `idempotent_replay=true` when the returned nonce differs (`E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/spend_lease.go:168`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/spend_lease.go:206`). But `admissionAuthorizeBody` intentionally omits `invocation_nonce`: receipt and boot proof replace it (`E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/spend_lease.go:232`). The router stores the original request's `body.invocation_nonce` (normally absent/None for Stage C), then returns that stored value on replay, not the enclave's current nonce (`R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:1945`, `R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:3038`). Thus reserve can commit, lose its response, and return a correct marked replay on a byte-identical retry, only for the decoder to turn it into local **409 `idempotency_replay` before marker validation**. Speculation is discarded and the committed hold can reap unbilled. Chat uses this very seam (`E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/stage_c.go:170`); this is not introduced by decide.

Chosen fix: keep the canonical Stage C body nonce-free and use the signed admission receipt as the replay identity **only for a fully validated marked response owned by the same live invocation**. Do not weaken ordinary nonce checks or accept every admission replay. Refactor shared decode/Stage C validation into one ordered acceptance decision:

1. Establish one `WithAuthorizationInvocation` scope at request entry, shared by prepare, reserve and transport retries. Bind the newly prepared immutable plan to that invocation's local owner identity, attempt key, exact receipt/hash, lease/generation/boot/workspace/key/policy and frozen candidates before speculation. Register at most one plan per invocation/attempt key and atomically enter its reserve operation once before dispatch; reject plan import/rebinding into another invocation, reconstruction from caller-supplied receipts, repeated reserve calls or a second dispatch claim. The current context fallback creates a new invocation when none is present, so preparation/reserve must explicitly share the owner rather than rely on that fallback (`E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/authorization_retry.go:50`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/authorization_retry.go:60`).
2. Marshal/sign once and retry the identical bytes, receipt and boot proof within that reserve operation and deadline. Do not prepare again, decrement capacity again or generate a replacement receipt on a transport retry. The receipt binds the idempotency scope and monotone remaining allocation (`E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:184`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:218`). Router replay must retain its stored-receipt equality/scope-conflict checks, including receipt-less replay protection after flag rollback (`R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_authorize.py:465`, `R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:1936`). A different invocation's newly allocated receipt cannot acquire the old authorization.
3. For a marked response, validate `accepted=true`, the exact owned receipt hash, nonempty authorization ID, bounded remaining capacity, identity and ordered dispatch/billing fields (including the proposed alias envelope) **before** allowing a receipt-based replay exception. Only this branch may disregard the absent/different stored invocation nonce: proof is the matching receipt plus local invocation ownership, not nonce equality. Apply the same full checks to first acceptance and replay, retain the original authorization/control-plane endpoint, and only reduce observed capacity. The existing marker checks provide the base (`E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/stage_c.go:196`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/stage_c.go:223`).
4. Atomically consume the invocation's key/plan acceptance claim once, after validation and before adopting a result or dispatching a replacement. Keep that tombstone for the invocation lifetime even after `Admission.Release` releases the in-flight map. Transport retries happen before this single successful claim; later reserve/authorize calls cannot create another execution using a replay. This extends, rather than removes, the existing `invocation.claim` defense (`E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/spend_lease.go:210`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/authorization_retry.go:81`). An unmarked response has no receipt-based exemption: a fresh ordinary response may proceed to isolated redispatch, but an ordinary/unmarked replay must pass the existing nonempty local nonce equality and unused-claim checks. A process restart or different invocation cannot recover dispatch rights merely by supplying the same idempotency key or copying a receipt.

This recovers the lost acknowledgement inside the original live invocation; it is not a cached-answer API or durable recovery across restarts. Never refund or settle a different invocation's authorization on a conflict. No missing/malformed marker may be promoted to accepted because the nonce matches.

### A billing consequence even before async settlement

Stage C's estimate is a reservation, not an exact price. Spend-lease settlement deliberately caps the customer charge at `min(allocated_micro, estimated_microdollars)`; actual cost above it is absorbed, not charged later (`R:src/trusted_router/services/spend_lease_settlement.py:94`). Therefore adding decide to spend leases can change the charge even with synchronous settle. Hosted's ordinary estimate can be below vendor-reported input; native estimates omit some provider formatting/schema overhead and cannot prove token counts. Do not advertise exact billing equivalence for this extension.

For Step 1 retain existing metering and decision-50 clamp; expose estimate/actual/clamp deltas and require Decision 1 approval **before enabling decide binding**, including for ordinary cold/miss/synchronous authorizations. Issuance-only shadow with the independent decide binding gate off does not authorize this billing exception. Do not inflate usage to the reservation or retrofit an overage bill. A later conservative-reservation variant could allocate a separately represented worst-case cost while preserving actual usage and pricing tiers, but changing `estimated_input_tokens` to a byte ceiling blindly would affect applicability. That requires shared estimator/schema tests, not an undocumented optimization. The hard bound required for Step 2 is stronger than Step 1's estimator.

### Decision: opt-in exception, not transparent reordering

### Queue and durable outbox are different guarantees

Current `settlementRetries` has capacity 1024, defaults to six attempts, is process memory, may drop its oldest job to enqueue a new one, and eventually gives up. Shutdown draining helps only graceful exits. It compacts jobs by stripping attribution; its worker calls `Settle` directly and does not replay `settleAndBroadcast`'s content broadcast. Native enqueues on initial settle failure; hosted's direct settle currently does not. The transport's own three retries/28-second budget are also not durable storage. (`E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/settlement_retry.go:15`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/settlement_retry.go:85`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/settlement_retry.go:112`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/settlement_retry.go:195`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/settlement_retry.go:269`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/settlement_retry.go:12`.)

Do not call existing `Enqueue` and equate its true return with a retained billing obligation. Add a decide-specific, bounded **non-evicting** handoff class with admission tickets acquired before speculation. Reserve capacity per attempt before dispatch, with at most one customer finalization owner for its authorization. On unmarked redispatch, retire speculative ownership and transfer the ticket to the fresh execution; discarded expense records do not become billing jobs. Transfer that execution's immutable usage, authorization, selected endpoint, fixed request ID, request-log/client context and required attribution before reply, and release the slot only on terminal classification or explicit recorded loss. A full/disabled handoff class means blocking settle for that request. Use a bounded worker pool and per-attempt deadlines so one hung settle cannot block the entire class; the ticket count covers queued and in-flight jobs. Never let ordinary retry pressure evict an already-replied obligation; exhausted retries occupy the loss budget and trip the async circuit breaker. Keep prompts, answers and provider secrets out of billing jobs; attribution retention needs the existing privacy policy and bounded sizes. Initially exclude broadcast-dependent requests unless equivalent worker behavior is tested. Add the hosted repair path as well.

Router outbox durability begins only when the settle handler commits its insert; it freezes resolved cost and settlement body and protects the hold from reaping. It does not know a result still queued inside an enclave. Its drain has a 60-second initial delay, so even durable intent is not immediate ledger/generation visibility (`R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:4038`, `R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:4073`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_authorize.py:1104`).

Handle outcomes semantically, not merely HTTP 200:

| Router outcome | Worker/accounting interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `finalized` | Terminal money operation committed; record outcome/cost/generation. |
| `intent_durable` | Router owns durable retry responsibility; transfer out of enclave queue, monitor outbox to terminal. Do not claim final charge/generation yet. |
| `already_finalized` | Inspect finalization outcome: it may be settled **or refunded**. Never charge again. An already-refunded completed call is a lost settlement. |
| `reaped_snapshot` | Reaper won; late actual usage does not replace its charge. Record `settle_lost`; do not broadcast this answer as that snapshot generation. |
| Error/timeout/missing or unknown disposition | Bounded idempotent retries against original authority; then explicit unresolved/loss accounting and async breaker, never a fresh charge. |

The router emits these dispositions and finalization outcomes (`R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:4638`, `R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:4677`, `R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:4705`). Enclave already treats reaped snapshots and `AlreadySettled && !Settled` as lost, and suppresses broadcasts without a generation or after loss (`E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/stage_d.go:402`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/settlement.go:38`). Do not use the disposition lookup alone as proof an outbox row exists: `_current_disposition` returns `intent_durable` for an unsettled record without checking the outbox. A timed-out settle needs retry or positive durable-intent evidence, not that inference.

### Crash cases and money at risk

Today the ordinary reaper releases stranded holds with `success=False`: **unbilled**, even if the provider ran. Stage D snapshot booking requires its flag, a started marker and pricing snapshot; decide has neither Stage D cohort eligibility nor its heartbeat snapshots. Merely enabling `TR_REAP_SNAPSHOT_BOOKING_ENABLED` does not protect decide. The checked-in rollout sets this flag true, while the Settings default is false; neither changes decide's exclusion. (`R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_authorize.py:1089`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_authorize.py:1301`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_authorize.py:1409`, `R:src/trusted_router/config.py:974`, `R:scripts/deploy/rollout.sh:690`.)

| Failure boundary | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Death after reserve, before provider completion | Existing stranded hold; unbilled reaping, possible provider expense. |
| Death after answer/handoff, before router outbox commit | New Step 1b exposure: delivered valid answer, lost in-memory usage, no durable settle intent; reaper refunds. |
| Death after outbox commit, before acknowledgement | Durable router repair survives; retry same authorization, no duplicate customer charge. |
| Reaper wins before late settle | Finalized refund today for decide; late settle cannot resurrect a charge. A future snapshot extension would instead preserve snapshot charge and report lost final usage. |

Let P_i be the ordinary priced usage for completed billable attempt i, A_i its lease allocation/estimated cap, and C_i = min(P_i, A_i) the spend-lease charge. One lost finalization loses **C_i microdollars of collectible revenue**, at most A_i. One native request can lose C_1 + C_2 if both completed attempts are still pending; a hosted request has one successful billable attempt. Separately record decision-50 undercharge max(0, P_i − A_i). Provider invoices V_i, including failed/discarded fallbacks, are a separate expense; customer lease caps do not prove V_i ≤ A_i. Do not add revenue loss and invoice expense as if they were the same accounting quantity.

Concrete scale: Jev's checked-in $0.042/M input rate means 1,000 billed tokens cost 42 microdollars = **$0.000042**; 32,000 cost 1,344 microdollars = **$0.001344** before a lower lease clamp. Its actual caller-charge ceiling from code is `round_half_up(0.042 × (state_bytes + question_json_bytes + 4096))` microdollars, subject to the positive-charge floor and A_i. 32k is the catalog context size, **not a proven absolute charged-token bound**: the handler clamps against bytes plus template allowance. Native must use its selected endpoint's input/output/cache rates and actual usage; 32,768 max output tokens alone does not bound its dollars. (`R:src/trusted_router/catalog_data.py:3161`, `E:enclave-go/cmd/enclave/decide.go:511`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/estimator.go:79`.)

Proposed initial async limits: **A_i ≤ 10,000 microdollars ($0.01) per attempt, at most 100 outstanding attempt tickets per enclave**. Thus at most $0.02 collectible revenue per two-attempt native request and $1 per enclave crash across that handoff class, further bounded by the capacities of the grants backing those obligations, including draining or expired grants still awaiting reconciliation. Count a worker's in-flight job, not only queued jobs. These are proposed rollout limits, not current configuration or a bound on arbitrary native vendor invoices. Set a separate $1 pilot-wide daily lost/unresolved-charge stop threshold, counting missing post-reply evidence conservatively; renewal and repeated crashes make a per-lease cap insufficient for cumulative loss. This is an operational stop threshold, not a hard global loss ceiling: detection delay and other enclaves' outstanding tickets add exposure. A hard $1 global ceiling would require partitioned, durably tracked risk tickets and withholding their renewal until reconciliation. Require an external observer because an enclave can lose its own counters on death. Any tighter invoice bound needs provider-specific caps and evidence before expansion.

Do not “fix” the crash gap by charging every expired decide allocation at its estimate: invalid hosted answers must refund, unused reservations are not consumption, and native incomplete generations refund. Exact eventual charging requires durable completed usage before reply (a synchronous durable handoff/round trip) or a new reviewed recovery/billing policy. Expanding Stage D to non-streaming decide is such a proposal, not a flag flip; an early heartbeat proves started input, not a valid delivered decision.

### What a cap can and cannot bound

For each boot-bound grant j with escrow L_j, allocate each attempt a conservative **hard upper bound B_i** before starting it, enforce `sum B_i ≤ L_j`, and never recycle decrements on refund, reserve refusal, process restart or lease transfer. If every delivered call's collectible charge is ≤ B_i, unreconciled delivered value is bounded by `sum_j L_j`, not “one request” and not necessarily one lease. A $1 grant permits up to $1 of such loss; four separately escrowed $1 grants permit up to $4. This is an exposure bound, not proof the customer will be billed: escrow remains a hold unless the settlement protocol has durable evidence and authority to debit it.

Existing authoritative minting reserves escrow transactionally, fences generations, binds grants to boot/key/region and limits open predecessors (currently three). Local ledger allocations are monotone and finalization never returns lease capacity. Those are foundations, not a proof for offline release. (`R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_spend_lease_authorize.py:63`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_spend_lease_authorize.py:161`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_spend_lease_authorize.py:192`, `R:src/trusted_router/storage_gcp_spend_lease_authorize.py:243`, `R:src/trusted_router/spend_lease_state.py:891`.)

Do not assume the generic Settings default of $1 is the live cap: trust-tier policy can select $5/$25/$100, additionally constrained by credit headroom, and issuance records the actual signed cap (`R:src/trusted_router/config.py:936`, `R:src/trusted_router/trust_eligibility.py:37`, `R:src/trusted_router/routes/internal/gateway.py:1677`, `R:scripts/deploy/rollout.sh:819`). Step 2 needs an explicit lower decide-specific cap and aggregate workspace outstanding-escrow limit, independent of larger chat tiers. Initial recommendation, if eventually approved: no more than $1 total offline decide authority for the pilot, inclusive of draining predecessors; no automatic growth from repeated restarts or rollover.

Today router receipt admission refuses late allocations after lease expiry plus skew; reconciliation can close authority that never reached the router (`R:src/trusted_router/spend_lease_state.py:957`). Step 2 must either deliver every allocation before that deadline or retain escrow and accept cryptographically proven in-window admissions through a specified recovery window. The former cannot guarantee availability during an outage. The latter needs new close/reconcile semantics: boot-bound monotonic admission sequence, durable replayable receipts/usage, finite recovery horizon, explicit handling of missing records and no release of “unused” escrow while offline obligations may exist. A RAM queue or restartable local disk file is not automatically durable or rollback-resistant in an enclave. No retroactive debit on a different authority and no cap billing for unknown usage by default.

### Non-streaming cap enforcement

Do not send Stage D `stream=true` heartbeats for decide. For hosted input-only billing, reserve the byte-plus-template ceiling using the highest eligible endpoint rate and request fee, prove vendor metering/cancellation and fallback expense bounds, and reject optimization when the bound does not fit. ### Multiple enclaves, replay and revocation

A boot-bound token cannot be used by another boot under existing verification (`E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:173`, `E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:301`). But two boots for the same key can hold distinct grants or old/new generations. Offline old boots cannot observe a transfer/fence immediately. Each still-valid authority must have disjoint, retained escrow; subtract the grant's creating authorization and all online allocations as well as offline admissions before spending. Otherwise a fresh local counter can spend capacity the router has already allocated. Fence/predecessor limits and the current downward-only `ObserveReserve` are not replacements for this accounting proof.

Current local dedupe is only an in-flight process map; cross-enclave arbitration occurs at reserve, and replay checks prevent accepting a second invocation only once their response is received (with the shared receipt-aware fix above required for legitimate retries) (`E:enclave-go/internal/spendlease/state.go:184`, `E:enclave-go/internal/trustedrouter/spend_lease.go:206`). Step 2 can return two answers for the same idempotency key before that arbitration, even though settlement ultimately charges once. To retain existing idempotency semantics, require a proven single offline owner for a key/epoch with non-overlapping validity windows and local replay retention through the recovery horizon, or retain online arbitration for such requests. Single-region routing affinity alone is not ownership. Cross-boot cloning, counter reset and restored snapshots must not recreate spendable authority.

Key revocation, billing pause, price/route removal or a newly imposed key limit may be invisible until grant expiry. Treat the stale-authority window and maximum retained escrow as explicit accepted risks; do not imply the ordinary router's immediate checks still happen before every success. Stop issuing/renewing on pause or reconciliation staleness; cap cumulative loss across grants and time, not only per boot.
